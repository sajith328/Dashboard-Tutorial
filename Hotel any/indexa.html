Dhokla: A steamed savory cake made from fermented rice and chickpea batter, often served with chutneys. Khaman: Similar to dhokla but made from gram flour, it’s spongy and typically garnished with mustard seeds, green chilies, and coriander. Bhutte ka kees

Bhutte Ka Kees is a cherished street food from Gujarat, known for its unique flavor and texture. This dish, made from grated corn, is a testament to the culinary creativity of the region. Let’s delve into the origins, preparation, and cultural significance of this delightful dish.

Ingredients and Preparation

The primary ingredient of Bhutte Ka Kees is fresh corn, which is grated to achieve a coarse texture. The grated corn is then cooked with a blend of spices, milk, and sometimes a hint of sugar, resulting in a creamy and aromatic dish. Here’s a brief overview of the preparation process:

nutritional value

Bhutte Ka Kees is not only delicious but also nutritious. Corn is a good source of dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals. The use of milk adds calcium and protein, making this dish a wholesome snack. The spices used in the preparation also offer various health benefits, such as aiding digestion and boosting immunity.

Khandvi

Preparation and Ingredients

Khandvi, also known as Patuli, Dahivadi, or Suralichi Vadi, is a popular snack from the Indian states of Gujarat and Maharashtra. This savory treat is made from gram flour (besan) and yogurt, which are cooked together to form a smooth batter. The batter is then spread thinly, cut into strips, and rolled into bite-sized pieces. These rolls are typically garnished with mustard seeds, sesame seeds, grated coconut, and fresh coriander. and is a savory snack in the Maharashtrian cuisine as well as in the Gujarati cuisine of India. ghatiya

Fry the Ghatiya

Ganthiya (Gujarati: ગાંઠિયા/ગાંઠીયા) are deep-fried Indian snacks made from chickpea flour.[1] Along with Khakhra, Fafda, Dhokla, and Khandvi (among others), they are among the most popular snacks originating from the Indian state of Gujarat. They are a popular teatime snack not only in Gujarat but across India and also among NRIs across the world.[1][2] They are sometimes soft and not always crunchy like most other Indian snacks.[3] A flatter flakier version is called papdi ganthiya.[4] A sweeter version is called Mitha Ganthiya. Bhavnagar city of Gujarat is famous for its variety of Ganthiya. Ganthia is part of a category of snack food called Farsan.
